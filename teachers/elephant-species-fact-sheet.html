Elephant species fact sheet

Three living species

Elephants are the largest living land animals. There are three species alive today. Two live in Africa and one lives in Asia. All three are threatened.

Scientific nameLoxodonta africanaLoxodonta cyclotisElephas maximus
Where it livesEastern, central and southern Africa, in savanna, grassland, woodland and flood plainDense rainforest in central Africa and parts of West Africa13 countries in South and Southeast Asia, in forest, scrub and grassland

SizeThe largest: adult males average about 3 to 3.2 m at the shoulderThe smallest of the threeIn between; males larger than females
FoodGrazes and browsesBrowses, and eats a lot of fruitBrowses and grazes, changing with the season

Latest published estimateAbout 415,000 African elephants of both species combined (2016). No newer separate savanna total yet.About 135,690 in surveyed areas (2024 status report)About 48,323 to 51,680 (2018 data, published 2020)

Three things every elephant needs

  • Food. Adults eat roughly 75 to 150 kg of plants a day and spend about 16 hours a day feeding (San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance).
  • Water. A wild adult needs about 189 liters a day (Smithsonian's National Zoo).
  • Family. Females and young live in family groups led by an older female called the matriarch. Adult males mostly live apart.
