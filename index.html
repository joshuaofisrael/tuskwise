Elephants, explained clearly and honestly

There are three living elephant species: the African savanna elephant, the African forest elephant and the Asian elephant. All three are threatened. The IUCN lists the savanna and Asian elephants as Endangered and the forest elephant as Critically Endangered. TuskWise explains how to tell them apart, how elephant families work, what science says about their memory and intelligence, where they live, and how to see them without harming them, with sources on every page.

Quick facts

  • Elephant pregnancy lasts about 22 months, the longest of any animal (why it takes so long).
  • The trunk is a fused nose and upper lip with no bones, built from about 150,000 muscle fascicles, according to San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance.
  • Tusks are elongated incisor teeth. In Asian elephants usually only males have visible tusks.
  • African elephant family herds are led by a matriarch, usually the oldest female; adult males mostly live alone or in loose bachelor groups.
  • Wild African elephants appear to address each other with individually specific, name like calls (Pardo and colleagues, 2024).
