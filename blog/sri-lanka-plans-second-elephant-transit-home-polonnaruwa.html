Sri Lanka plans a second Elephant Transit Home in Polonnaruwa

Published 26 October 2026 · Last reviewed 26 October 2026 · By the TuskWise team

Sri Lanka has decided to set up a second Elephant Transit Home, a rescue and rehabilitation centre for orphaned and injured elephant calves, at Kalahagala near Polonnaruwa, the Polonnaruwa District Secretary said on 24 October 2026. About 15 hectares of Forest Department land will be allocated. The country's only such centre today is at Udawalawe in the south, a journey of 7 to 9 hours for calves rescued in the north. No construction timeline has been announced.

What was announced

Polonnaruwa District Secretary Sujantha Ekanayake said on 24 October 2026 that a new Elephant Transit Home will be established at Kalahagala, with about 15 hectares of land from the Department of Forest Conservation set aside for it. The site was picked after a survey by an expert committee of veterinarians, forest officers and public representatives, News 1st and Newswire reported.

Why it matters for calves

Sri Lanka's only Elephant Transit Home is at Udawalawe, in the south of the island. Calves found in the Northern and North Central provinces, separated from their mothers or hurt in human-elephant conflict, floods or train collisions, must travel 7 to 9 hours to get there, which officials said puts injured calves at serious risk. Newswire reported that the new centre should cut that journey to about 1 to 2 hours before treatment can begin.

Former Director General of Wildlife Conservation Dr Sumith Pilapitiya told Newswire the centre would also let calves be rehabilitated and released into reserves in their own region, helping keep Sri Lanka's three main Asian elephant subpopulations naturally separate. Officials also expect local jobs and tourism, for example from supplying food for the calves.

Context

Human-elephant conflict is a major issue in Sri Lanka, and the recent death of the tusker Kavantissa after a gunshot wound kept the issue in the news. No construction or opening dates for Kalahagala have been given yet; TuskWise will update this post when they are. Frequently asked questions

What is an Elephant Transit Home?

It is a centre that takes in orphaned or injured wild elephant calves, treats and raises them, and later releases them back into protected areas. Sri Lanka's existing Elephant Transit Home is at Udawalawe.

Where will the new Elephant Transit Home be?

At Kalahagala in the Polonnaruwa District of Sri Lanka's North Central Province, on about 15 hectares of land belonging to the Department of Forest Conservation.

Why is a second centre needed?

Calves rescued in the Northern and North Central provinces currently face 7 to 9 hours of transport to Udawalawe, which officials say puts injured calves' lives at serious risk. The new site is expected to cut that to about 1 to 2 hours.

When will it open?

No construction or opening dates had been announced as of 26 October 2026.
