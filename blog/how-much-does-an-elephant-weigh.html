How much does an elephant weigh?

Published 26 October 2026 · Last reviewed 26 October 2026 · By the TuskWise team

It depends on the species and the sex. In good condition, an average fully grown male African savanna elephant, about 3.2 metres tall at the shoulder, weighs around 6,000 kg (6 tonnes), while an average female, about 2.6 metres tall, weighs around 3,000 kg. An average Asian bull weighs about 4,000 kg and an Asian cow about 2,700 kg. African forest elephants are the smallest, with an average bull estimated at about 2,000 kg. These figures come from a 2016 review of elephant shoulder heights and body masses by Asier Larramendi.

The short answer

An adult elephant usually weighs somewhere between 2 and 6 tonnes, depending on species and sex, and the biggest bulls go well beyond that. The most careful modern figures come from a 2016 study by palaeontologist Asier Larramendi in Acta Palaeontologica Polonica. To estimate the weight of extinct mammoths and mastodons, he first had to pin down how much living elephants weigh at a given shoulder height, using data from wild and well-fed populations rather than overweight captive animals.

Elephant weight by species

Here are the average body masses Larramendi gives for fully grown elephants in good condition:

  • African savanna (bush) elephant: bulls about 6,000 kg at around 320 cm shoulder height; cows about 3,000 kg (not pregnant) at around 260 cm.
  • Asian elephant: bulls about 4,000 kg at around 275 cm; fully grown cows around 2,700 kg.
  • African forest elephant: no study has weighed them systematically, but an average bull of about 220 cm at the shoulder should be around 2,000 kg, because its body shape is very similar to the savanna elephant's.

So the savanna elephant is the heaviest land animal alive today, and a big savanna bull weighs roughly as much as three family cars. The forest elephant, which looks like a smaller, rounder-eared cousin, is a third of that weight. You can compare the species side by side in our African vs Asian elephant guide.

Are Asian elephants heavier than African ones?

A common belief is that Asian elephants are much heavier than African elephants of the same height. Larramendi argues that this idea mostly comes from weights recorded in zoo and circus animals, some of which were 60 to 85% heavier than healthy elephants of the same shoulder height: in other words, obese. Compared fairly, the two are close. Asian elephants have a broader pelvis and a more compact body, while African elephants have a longer body, and at 300 cm shoulder height both species come out at about 5,000 kg.

Why bulls are so much heavier than cows

Elephants show strong sexual dimorphism: males are far bigger than females. Savanna bulls are on average about 23% taller than cows, and because weight rises faster than height, they end up about twice as heavy. In Asian elephants the gap is smaller, with males about 15% taller than females.

Part of the difference comes from how long each sex keeps growing. A 2015 study led by Hannah Mumby followed semi-captive working Asian elephants in Myanmar, whose ages and measurements are recorded over their lives. Neither sex kept growing taller throughout life. Females finished most of their height growth by about 15 years and males by about 21. Females also levelled off in weight, but males kept gaining weight until over 50 years of age, and neither sex showed a decline in weight with age. An old bull is heavy because he has filled out, not because he is still getting taller. Read more about the elephant life cycle in how long elephants live.

How heavy was the biggest elephant ever?

The largest accurately measured African bush elephant was a bull shot in Angola in 1974. From its forefoot circumference of 180 cm, its shoulder height is put at about 396 cm, some 25% taller than an average bull. Older books often quoted a weight of over 12 tonnes. Using a growth curve in which weight does not rise quite as fast as simple scaling would suggest, Larramendi predicted it weighed about 10.4 tonnes, still close to twice the weight of an average bull.

The same study suggests that some extinct relatives dwarfed today's elephants. Straight-tusked elephants of the genus Palaeoloxodon, and the mastodon relative Mammut borsoni, may have reached or surpassed the size of the giant hornless rhinos once thought to be the largest land mammals ever.

What all that weight means for an elephant

Being so large brings problems as well as protection. Because volume grows faster than surface area, a big body makes a lot of heat but has relatively little skin to lose it through. A 2012 study in PLoS ONE noted that elephants have the greatest need for heat loss of any land animal for exactly this reason, and found that even their sparse body hair helps, increasing heat loss by more than 5% and by up to 23% in low wind. Big ears and mud baths help too.

All that mass also has to be fuelled, which is why elephants spend so much of the day feeding (see how much elephants eat and drink), and carried, which shapes how they move: elephants never get all four feet off the ground at once, as explained in how fast elephants can run.

The bottom line

A typical adult savanna bull weighs about 6 tonnes, a savanna cow about 3 tonnes, an Asian bull about 4 tonnes, an Asian cow about 2.7 tonnes, and a forest bull about 2 tonnes. Record bulls can top 10 tonnes. For more on how elephant bodies work, see our elephant anatomy guide or browse the elephant FAQ.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an African elephant weigh?

An average fully grown male African savanna (bush) elephant in good condition weighs about 6,000 kg at roughly 3.2 m shoulder height, and an average adult female about 3,000 kg at 2.6 m, according to a 2016 review in Acta Palaeontologica Polonica. African forest elephants are much smaller, with an average bull estimated at about 2,000 kg.

How much does an Asian elephant weigh?

An average adult male Asian elephant of about 2.75 m shoulder height weighs around 4,000 kg in optimal condition, and an average fully grown female around 2,700 kg. Heavier figures often quoted come largely from overweight zoo and circus animals.

What was the heaviest elephant ever recorded?

The largest accurately measured African bush elephant was a bull shot in Angola in 1974, estimated at about 3.96 m at the shoulder. A 2016 analysis predicted it weighed about 10.4 tonnes, around 1.9 tonnes less than older estimates of 12 tonnes or more.

Are male elephants heavier than females?

Yes, by a lot. African savanna bulls are on average about 23% taller than cows and roughly twice as heavy. In Asian elephants males are about 15% taller than females. Male Asian elephants also keep gaining weight until past age 50, long after they stop growing taller.

Do elephants keep growing all their lives?

Not in height. A long-term study of semi-captive Asian elephants in Myanmar found that females finish almost all their height growth by about 15 years and males by about 21. Females also stop gaining weight, but males keep putting on weight until over age 50.

Is the elephant the heaviest land animal?

Yes. The African savanna elephant is the largest and heaviest living land animal. Some extinct relatives were bigger still: the 2016 study estimated that fossil elephants such as Palaeoloxodon namadicus may have rivalled or exceeded the largest prehistoric rhinos.
