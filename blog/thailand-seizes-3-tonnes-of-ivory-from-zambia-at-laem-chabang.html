Thailand seizes 3.1 tonnes of ivory hidden in timber from Zambia

Published 30 October 2026 · Last reviewed 30 October 2026 · By the TuskWise team

Thai authorities have seized more than 3.1 tonnes of African elephant ivory hidden inside timber shipped from Zambia, in what Thailand's customs chief called one of the biggest ivory seizures the department has made. The tusks were found in two containers at Laem Chabang port, sealed in wax inside hollowed-out planks, and were announced at a press conference on 26 October 2026.

What happened

Acting on intelligence shared by international partners, Thai customs officers held two suspicious containers at Laem Chabang port in Chonburi province. The cargo had been imported from Zambia and, according to Thai officials, was due to be sent on to a third country. It was declared as 568 planks of sawn timber with a net weight of 56,000 kg. When officers inspected it, they found that 32 of the planks had been hollowed out, filled with ivory and wildlife remains, and sealed with wax.

Thailand's Customs Department, its Department of Special Investigation (DSI) and the Department of National Parks, Wildlife and Plant Conservation announced the find at a joint press conference at the port on Monday 26 October 2026. The Bangkok Post reported that the containers had been impounded the previous month, and that the announcement was delayed because each wax-coated tusk had to be boiled and cleaned.

What was found

According to the DSI and customs figures, the haul included:

  • 469 tusks and tusk sections weighing 3,042.67 kg
  • 1,038 ivory fragments weighing 66.20 kg, bringing the total ivory to 3,108.87 kg
  • 335 elephant tail hairs (0.18 kg)
  • one wildlife carcass, which the Bangkok Post reported was a lion

The seized items were valued at about 155 million baht. The customs director-general told reporters it was one of the biggest ivory seizures the department has made, and credited information from the Wildlife Justice Commission and the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime. Wildlife officials have taken DNA samples, which can help show where the elephants were killed, and the DSI's natural resources crime division is investigating who was behind the shipment. No one has been publicly charged.

Zambia's response

Zambia's Ministry of Tourism said in early October that preliminary information suggested the consignment might have originated in Zambia but that this had not been established. Earlier Zambian statements, reported by Zambia Monitor, referred to 446 tusks; the Thai authorities' final count is 469 tusks and sections. The ministry's permanent secretary said investigators were reviewing permits, timber export records and customs declarations, that a preliminary inspection of the government's ivory strongroom showed no sign that ivory had been stolen, and that Zambia, as a land-linked country, may have been used as a transit route.

Why it matters

Three tonnes of ivory represents a large number of dead elephants, and big shipments like this usually point to organised trafficking networks rather than lone poachers. Poaching for ivory remains one of the main threats to Africa's elephants, as we explain in our elephant conservation guide and our look at how many elephants are left. It has even changed elephants themselves: in some heavily poached populations, more females are now born without tusks. DNA results from this seizure could help investigators trace where the ivory came from. We will update this story when officials release findings.

Frequently asked questions

How much ivory was seized at Laem Chabang?

According to Thailand's Department of Special Investigation and Customs Department, officers found 469 tusks and tusk sections weighing 3,042.67 kg plus 1,038 ivory fragments weighing 66.20 kg, a total of 3,108.87 kg. They also found 335 elephant tail hairs and one wildlife carcass. The haul was valued at about 155 million baht.

How was the ivory hidden?

The two containers were declared as 568 planks of sawn timber weighing 56,000 kg. Thirty-two of the planks had been hollowed out, packed with ivory and sealed with wax, which the customs chief said was meant to make the tusks harder to spot on X-ray.

Did the ivory come from Zambia?

The shipment came from Zambia, but where the elephants were killed has not been established. Thai wildlife officials are running DNA tests on the tusks. Zambia's government says it is checking export and shipping documents, that a preliminary check of its ivory strongroom found no sign of theft, and that the country may have been used as a transit route.

Why is ivory trafficking still a problem?

International commercial trade in elephant ivory is banned under CITES, but demand in parts of Asia keeps organised smuggling going. Poaching for ivory is one of the main threats to African savanna and forest elephants, alongside habitat loss and conflict with people.
