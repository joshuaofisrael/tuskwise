What happened

On 17 October 2026, Perak's state environment committee chairman Teh Kok Lim told reporters in Ipoh that the state forestry department and the Department of Wildlife and National Parks (Perhilitan) are working together to look for sites for elephant food banks and buffer zones. The aim is to give elephants enough to eat near their natural habitat so they are less likely to move into villages and farms. He said Perak does not yet have a food bank, unlike Terengganu, Pahang, Johor and Kelantan, that elephants may not stay inside designated areas, and that no final decision has been made. He repeated on 19 October that suitable locations still have to be identified.

The raids behind the proposal

Farmers at Bukit Bangkong, near Chemor, reported elephants in their orchards on 27 September, 3 October and 11 October, affecting about 9.7 hectares and 12 farmers growing crops such as durian, coconut, jackfruit and banana. On 20 October Perhilitan said it had received six reports of incursions around Sungai Siput, by a herd it estimated at three to eight elephants from the Korbu Forest Reserve, part of a larger group of about 50 animals that has split up. Oil palm smallholders there say about 80 hectares of crops have been damaged since early September. Perhilitan says it is monitoring the herd and will focus on conflict hotspots, early reporting and community awareness.

How food banks work elsewhere in Malaysia

Johor already plants food banks along elephant movement corridors. As Free Malaysia Today describes them, these are not feeding stations but planted plots, with banana and Napier grass, inside designated habitat. The programme began with three sites of more than a hectare each, and the federal government announced plans in July for 21 more in the state.

Why it matters

Asian elephants are endangered, and conflict with farmers is one of the biggest threats to them. Crop raiding is not random. In a classic study in southern India, Raman Sukumar found that crops gave elephants more protein, calcium and sodium than wild grasses and that raids rose with the planting season, which is why fences and scare tactics alone rarely solve it. Whether food banks work in Perak will depend on where they go and whether they are paired with protected corridors. We will update this post if the state confirms a plan.
