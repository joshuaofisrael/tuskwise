How we write

Every page is written for TuskWise by the TuskWise editorial team. That team name is the byline on our articles. We do not use a fake personal author, and we do not claim to be elephant researchers, veterinarians or people with field credentials we do not have. We do not copy text from other sites. Where evidence is thin or scientists disagree, we say so. If you spot an error, tell us through the contact form.

There is no second editorial policy page. This document is the policy. The About page links here, and the footer on every page does too.

How we source facts

Facts come from published primary sources. For conservation status and numbers we use the IUCN Red List and IUCN Specialist Group status reports, plus national surveys when they publish new methods or totals. For anatomy, behavior and diet we prefer peer reviewed papers (we check DOIs) and fact sheets from accredited zoos such as the Smithsonian's National Zoo and San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance. News posts need at least two independent sources before we publish.

Each guide lists its sources at the bottom of the page. We give the year of an assessment or estimate next to the figure when we can. Population counts from different years or methods are not added together as if they were one census. We do not invent numbers, quotes or study findings. When we refresh a page, we recheck the cited Red List pages and note the date we checked.

AI-generated images

Some images are AI-generated illustrations made to look like realistic wildlife photographs. Each one is labelled as an AI-generated image in its alt text or caption. They show typical elephant scenes, not a real individual animal, a real person or a real event, and we do not use them to illustrate news stories. Real photographs are credited on the photo credits page. Full detail is in our image policy.
